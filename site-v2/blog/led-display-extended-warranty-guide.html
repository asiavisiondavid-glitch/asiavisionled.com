Extended Warranty in the US Market

In North America an extended warranty is usually bought by an owner or an operator rather than by the reseller, and it is frequently written into the project documents as a line item.

Three features distinguish the US version. It is a service product, not a longer clock — the buyer is paying for response and parts availability for a defined period, so the term without the response commitment is not what was purchased. It is normally priced against the installed value rather than the equipment price, because the cost of an outage is what the buyer is insuring against. And it usually requires proof that the routine maintenance was carried out, since a coverage that pays out regardless of maintenance is a coverage the seller cannot price.

Two points for the buyer. Confirm whether parts are held locally for the extended period, because an extension that relies on overseas stock does not deliver the response it implies. And confirm the transferability, since a US owner may sell the building during the term.
