What is an led display site survey? An led display site survey is the visit to the site before the installation, where the installer measures the space and checks the structure, the power, and the access. The survey prevents the surprises at the install. This 2026 guide explains it.

The installation rarely goes wrong because of the screen; it goes wrong because of the site. The wall is not flat, the power is not where it should be, or the access is too narrow. The survey finds these issues before the install, when the fix is cheap.

This led display site survey guide is written for the installers and the project managers. It explains the measurements, the structure, the power, the access, and the survey report.

What Does the Survey Measure?

The survey measures the mounting area, the viewing distances, and the environment. The width and the height decide the screen size; the distance decides the pitch; the light and the weather decide the brightness and the IP rating. The measurements set the specification.

ItemWhat to MeasureWhy It Matters
Mounting areaWidth, height, flatnessThe screen size and the frame
Viewing distanceNearest and furthestThe pixel pitch
Ambient lightDay and nightThe brightness and the dimming
ClimateRain, wind, sunThe IP rating and the cooling

The survey should also photograph the site, so the office team sees the conditions. The photos support the plan and the design. The measured and photographed site is the basis of the accurate design.

What Should the Survey Measure?

FindingAction

The survey checks the mounting surface: the wall, the truss, or the ground. The structure must carry the screen's weight, so the survey notes the material and the condition. A weak wall may need the steel sub-frame or the different mounting.

  • The wall material and the thickness
  • The structural condition and the cracks
  • The available mounting points
  • The clearance behind the screen
  • The access for the install and the service
Site survey checklist:

✅ The structure assessed
✅ The power supply checked
✅ The access and the route checked

The Power and the Signal

The survey checks the power: the supply capacity, the location, and the circuit. The screen's load must fit the supply, so the survey notes the available power. A site with the limited power may need the supply upgrade or the smaller screen.

The survey also checks the signal: the source, the route, and the distance. The signal cable has a limit, so the survey notes the run. The source and the route decide the control plan for the installation and the operation.

The Access and the Route

The survey checks the access: the delivery route, the door, the lift, and the crane if the screen is high. A large screen that cannot reach the site, or the installation point, delays the whole project. The survey finds the access issues early.

The survey should also check the storage and the working space, so the crew has the room. The tight site needs the plan. The access and the space are parts of the installation the survey addresses.

The Survey Report

The survey produces the report, with the measurements, the photos, and the notes. The report informs the design, the quote, and the install plan. The documented survey is the reference for the whole project, so the office and the crew work from the same facts.

The report should also note the risks and the recommendations, such as the sub-frame or the power upgrade. The flagged risk lets the project plan the fix. The complete report prevents the surprise at the install, when the site issue would be the expensive to fix.

Common Survey Mistakes

The common mistakes are the skipped survey, the unmeasured wall, and the unchecked power. Others include the ignored access and the no photos. Each causes the delay or the rework at the installation, when the fix is the most expensive and the disruptive.

The remedy is to survey the site before the order, measure the key items, and document the report. The survey turns the installation from the gamble into the planned project, with the screen that fits the site and the crew that knows what to expect.

The Survey and the Screen Size

The survey's measurement decides the screen size, so the buyer should confirm the dimensions before the order. The screen that is too large does not fit the wall, and one too small leaves the gap. The accurate measurement is the basis of the correct screen for the space.

The size also interacts with the pitch and the viewing distance, as the survey notes. The measured room and the distance set the specification. The survey, informed by the measurements, produces the screen that fits the space and the audience and the budget.

The Survey and the Environment

The survey checks the environment: the light, the weather, the dust, and the temperature. The bright area needs the high brightness; the outdoor needs the IP rating; the hot site needs the cooling. The environment shapes the specification and the design.

The environment also affects the installation, such as the corrosion on the coast or the snow on the roof. The survey notes the conditions, so the plan accounts for them. The environment-aware survey prevents the screen that fails the weather or the fails the light.

The Survey and the Services

The survey checks the services: the power, the network, and the access. Each affects the install and the operation. The missing service delays the project, so the survey confirms them. The services are parts of the installation the survey addresses before the order.

The survey should also note the service's owner and the process, because the power or the network may need the arrangement with the landlord or the utility. The early check prevents the delay at the install. The service planning, informed by the survey, keeps the project on the schedule.

The Survey and the Risk

The survey is the risk assessment, because it finds the issues that could delay or the damage the project. The weak wall, the limited power, the narrow access, and the harsh environment are the risks. The survey, with the risk noted, lets the project plan the mitigation.

The risk should be recorded in the report, with the recommendation, so the buyer and the installer know the exposure. The documented risk informs the quote, the schedule, and the design. The survey, treated as the risk assessment, protects the project from the surprise at the install.

The Survey and the Design

The survey feeds the design, because the measurements, the structure, and the services inform the frame, the power, and the control. The designer works from the survey. The design without the survey is the guess, so the survey is the foundation of the accurate design and the smooth install.

The design should reference the survey, so the two agree. The buyer should review the design against the survey, so the issues are addressed. The survey and the design together make the plan that fits the site, and the installer works with the confidence in the facts.

The Survey and the Cost

The survey informs the cost, because the structure, the power, and the access affect the work and the budget. The weak wall needs the sub-frame; the limited power needs the upgrade; the hard access needs the crane. The survey, with the notes, gives the accurate quote.

The cost should reflect the survey, so the hidden work is included. The screen price alone misleads, when the structure and the power cost the more. The survey-informed quote is the accurate one, and the buyer compares the real cost rather than the screen price.

The Survey and the Team

The survey is best done by the team that will install the screen, because the installer sees the issues the office may miss. The crew that surveys the site knows the access, the power, and the structure when the install begins, which speeds the work and reduces the surprises on the day of the installation.

The team should include the structural and the electrical knowledge, or bring the specialists. The survey with the right people catches the risks at the source. The multi-skilled team, on the survey, makes the report complete and the plan realistic for the actual install and the operation of the finished screen.

The Survey and the Timing

The survey should happen before the order, so the findings inform the specification and the cost. The late survey, after the order, leaves the buyer committed to the screen that may not fit. The early survey is the foundation of the sound decision on the screen and the install.

The survey should also be repeated if the site changes or the time passes, because the conditions may shift. The fresh survey keeps the plan current. The buyer should treat the survey as the living document, the repeated before the install if the site or the project has changed.

Planning the Survey

The led display site survey measures the site and checks the structure, the power, and the access before the install. The survey sets the specification and the plan, so the installation goes smoothly and the surprises are avoided.

Installers who survey the site find the issues while they are cheap to fix. The survey is the first step of the successful install, and the report is the foundation of the design and the plan.
