What is a Vietnam government LED display tender? A Vietnam government LED display tender is a procurement run under the national law on bidding, advertised on the official portal and evaluated against published criteria, with bid security and capability requirements that shape the submission. This 2026 guide explains what bidders must prepare.

Public procurement in Vietnam is regulated by the law on bidding and increasingly conducted through the national portal. A government LED display tender is announced publicly, evaluated against published criteria, and awarded in a documented process that bidders can follow and, where necessary, challenge.

The process rewards preparation. Registration, capability evidence, and bid security all take time, and a bidder who starts when the notice appears is usually behind those who prepared earlier. Understanding the sequence before a tender is published is the first step.

Procurement Law and the Bidding Portal for a Vietnam Government LED Display Tender

Government procurement follows the national law on bidding, which sets out how contracts are advertised, evaluated, and awarded. Most tenders are published on the official portal, and bidders must be registered and qualified for the work before they can submit.

Register early. A company that begins the process when a tender appears is usually too late, because the administrative checks take time and the bidding window does not extend for them. Pre-registration and prequalification are part of preparing to win.

Read the tender documents completely before deciding to bid. A requirement that is easy to miss in a long document can disqualify a bid, and the cost of preparing a submission is wasted if a formal condition was not met.

Bid Security and Financial Requirements

A Vietnam government LED display tender normally requires bid security with the submission and a performance bond on award, usually a percentage of the contract value. Arrange the security through a bank early, because issuing it takes time and a late security disqualifies the bid.

Price the security cost and the documentation effort into the offer. A performance bond for a large screen supply carries a real cost, and a bidder who forgets it discovers the margin has gone after the award rather than before it.

Financial documents to prepare:
✅ Bid security issued before the deadline
✅ Performance bond arranged on award
✅ Security cost included in the price
✅ Bank reference and audited accounts
✅ Valid registration and capability evidence

Bid Documents and Technical Evidence

The bid package normally includes the company documents, the technical offer, the price, and the supporting evidence for both. Technical evidence should relate to the exact model offered, and a test report for a similar product does not satisfy the requirement.

DocumentPurposeCommon Failure

Technical offerSpecification complianceVague or unsupported claims
Test reportsEvidence for the offered modelReport for a different model

Prepare the technical file before the tender appears. A supplier who can produce test reports and references immediately is in a stronger position than one who must obtain them during the bidding window. Keep a bid library so each tender can be answered without starting from scratch.

Bid library contents:
✅ Company registration and certificates
✅ Test reports by model
✅ Reference project list with contacts
✅ Standard specification write-ups
✅ Service partner agreements

Evaluation of a Vietnam Government LED Display Tender

Evaluation usually scores technical compliance, price, experience, and capability, using criteria published in the tender. The evaluation is applied as published, and a bid that scores well overall can still fail if it misses a mandatory requirement.

CriterionWhat It MeasuresTypical Weight
Technical complianceMeets every stated requirement25 to 35 percent
PriceCost against the requirement25 to 35 percent
ExperienceSimilar projects delivered10 to 20 percent
CapabilityResources and local support10 to 20 percent
Service and sparesSupport across the screen life10 to 15 percent
Delivery programmeAbility to meet the date5 to 10 percent

Where capability and service carry weight, describe the specific commitment rather than a general intention. A named local partner, a stated response time, or a service facility in the country is verifiable, while a general statement is not.

Delivery and Acceptance

Public projects specify delivery, installation, commissioning, training, and warranty obligations, and the acceptance test may be defined by the entity rather than agreed later. Plan the delivery programme to include these obligations and add a buffer for customs clearance.

Tie the acceptance test to the payment milestones. A supplier who is paid only after the screen passes the specified test has an incentive to prepare properly, and the entity has a clear standard for accepting the work. Document the results with photographs and measurements.

Common Mistakes in Vietnamese Public Bids

  • Starting registration only when a tender appears
  • Arranging bid security too late to be issued
  • Offering a model with no matching test evidence
  • Using references that cannot be contacted
  • Leaving a mandatory requirement unanswered
  • Underestimating customs clearance time in the programme

Most of these are administrative failures rather than technical ones, which is what makes them frustrating. A supplier with a good product can lose a Vietnam government LED display tender on a missing document or a lapsed certificate, so the paperwork deserves the same attention as the specification, as discussed in the tender evaluation guide and the vendor scorecard guide.

Supplier Registration for a Vietnam Government LED Display Tender

Registration and capability assessment take time, so begin before a tender appears. A company that starts the process when the announcement is published is usually too late, because the administrative checks do not move faster for a deadline. Complete registration and keep the documents current.

Keep the registration documents updated as certificates and accounts expire. A lapsed document discovered at submission time can remove a bidder from a tender they were otherwise able to win, and the loss is administrative rather than technical.

Service Capability in Vietnamese Public Projects

Public projects often require after-sales capability within the country, because a screen installed in a government building must be supported locally. A bidder without a local service presence needs a partner who can attend site, hold spares, and respond within the stated time.

Describe the service arrangement in specific terms. A named partner, a stated response time, and a spare parts plan are verifiable, while a general commitment to support the customer is not. Specific commitments score during evaluation and can be checked at delivery.

Debrief after every tender, won or lost, and keep a bid library of the documents that change rarely. A supplier who treats a Vietnam government LED display tender as a learning opportunity compounds its advantage over one who treats each submission as an isolated event.

Keep a bid library so each tender can be answered without starting from scratch. The company documents, test reports, references, and capability evidence change rarely, and a library means the team spends its time on the project-specific content rather than reassembling the same paperwork.

A supplier who has completed several Vietnamese public projects understands the paperwork, the security requirements, and the local partnerships needed, and that accumulated experience is often worth more than a lower price in the evaluation.
