What does a Peru LED display importer need to know? Importing LED displays into Peru means registering with SUNAT, clearing goods through Callao, confirming whether an electrical safety technical regulation applies and how INACAL fits in, and specifying equipment for a coastal desert climate and a high-altitude interior. This 2026 guide explains the process.

Peru is one of the larger LED display markets in South America, with demand from retail, banking, mining, transport, stadiums, and events. Almost all professional screens are imported from China, and the country's trade is concentrated through a single dominant gateway at Callao.

A Peru LED display importer who confirms the registration and the technical regulation position in advance clears cargo without difficulty. The two factors that most often delay a project are the conformity question and the extreme range of climates between the coast and the highlands.

Customs Entry for a Peru LED Display Importer

Importers in Peru register with the national tax and customs administration, SUNAT, and operate under a RUC tax identification number that identifies the entity filing the declaration. Foreign trade entries are lodged electronically through the SUNAT customs system, and the importer of record carries legal responsibility for the accuracy of the entry.

A licensed customs agent files the declaration on behalf of the importer. The entry states the classification, the customs value, and the origin of each line, and duty and general sales tax are assessed on that basis.

Work with a customs agent who handles electronic goods. The classification of a display, its power supplies, its control cards, and its mounting steel differs, and each line carries its own tariff treatment and its own technical regulation position.

INACAL, Technical Regulations, and Peruvian Compliance

Peru develops national standards through the Instituto Nacional de Calidad, known as INACAL, which is the country's standards and accreditation body. INACAL publishes Peruvian technical standards, administers the national quality system, and accredits the certification bodies and testing laboratories that operate in Peru.

Product requirements for electrical goods are set out in technical regulations issued by the competent ministries, and conformity is demonstrated through a certificate issued by an accredited product certification body, known in Peru as an organismo de certificacion de productos. Confirm the position for the specific model rather than assuming a general rule applies, because the scope depends on the product and its use.

Keep the technical file after the goods are sold, because the authorities may request it during the life of the product and the same file supports a warranty claim. A file assembled at the order stage costs far less than one reconstructed later.

Duty, IGV, and the Landed Cost

Peru applies a tariff with a small number of ad valorem bands, with general sales tax, known as IGV, on top. Duty is calculated on the customs value, normally the transaction value adjusted to the agreed Incoterm, and the tax applies to the duty-paid value. A range of minor charges adds to the total.

General sales tax is recoverable for a registered business, so the effect is on cash flow rather than on final cost. Duty and any certification charge are not recoverable and belong in the cost calculation from the beginning.

Working With a Peruvian Customs Agent

Importers work through a licensed customs agent rather than filing directly. Choose an agent who clears electronic goods rather than general cargo, and confirm that the agent holds the licences required for the port and the type of operation you use.

Give the agent the complete document pack before dispatch. A shipment that reaches Callao with incomplete paperwork is held while the gap is filled, and storage charges accumulate for every day the container remains in the terminal, which is a large and busy one.

Specifying for Peruvian Conditions

Peru has one of the most varied climates of any importing market. The coast is a cool desert with almost no rainfall and persistent coastal fog, the highlands are cold and dry at altitude, and the eastern lowlands are hot and humid. The same specification will not suit every site.

For Peruvian sites, low winter temperatures make cabinet construction and sealing the deciding factors — the X Series uses standard 500×500mm cabinets with front/rear service, and the COB EF outdoor series is sealed against snow-melt and moisture ingress.

Seismic design is a specification point that buyers outside the region often overlook. Peru lies in a seismically active zone, so mounting structures for large screens must be engineered for movement, and the mounting design belongs in the technical file.

Logistics and Lead Times to Peru

Almost all containerised LED display cargo reaches Peru through Callao, the country's principal port and the gateway for Lima. The northern port of Paita and the southern port of Matarani handle regional traffic, and air freight lands at Jorge Chavez International Airport for urgent spare parts.

Add a buffer of at least two weeks beyond the quoted transit time. On a long route with transshipment small delays accumulate, and a screen tied to an opening date has no room to absorb them.

Common Mistakes Peru Importers Make

  • Ordering before the technical regulation position is confirmed
  • Assuming one specification suits coast, highland, and lowland alike
  • Ignoring the seismic requirement in the mounting design
  • Declaring a mixed shipment under one classification
  • Leaving the certification cost out of the landed cost
  • Failing to hold the technical file after delivery
  • Using a customs agent who does not clear electronic goods

Each mistake is inexpensive to avoid during planning and costly after the goods land. A Peru LED display importer who confirms the compliance position, engineers the mounting, and specifies for the actual site altitude and climate avoids most of them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What does a Peru LED display importer need before the first shipment?
A: Register with SUNAT to obtain a RUC number, appoint a licensed customs agent, confirm whether a technical regulation applies to the specific model, and prepare Spanish labelling and the technical file before dispatch.
Q: What is the role of INACAL in Peru?
A: INACAL is the Instituto Nacional de Calidad, Peru's standards and accreditation body. It publishes Peruvian technical standards and accredits the certification bodies and laboratories that issue conformity certificates for regulated products.
Q: Which port handles LED display imports into Peru?
A: Callao is the principal port and the gateway for Lima, handling almost all containerised project cargo. Paita in the north and Matarani in the south handle regional traffic, and urgent spare parts normally arrive by air.
Q: What specification suits Peruvian conditions?
A: The coast is a cool desert with fog and salt air, the highlands are cold and dry at altitude, and the lowlands are hot and humid. Specify corrosion protection on the coast, acclimatisation and UV stability at altitude, and sealed cabinets in the lowlands, plus seismically engineered mounting.
Q: How long does shipping from China to Peru take?
A: Allow approximately six to ten weeks in total, covering production, export formalities, a long sea leg, import clearance at Callao, and inland delivery. Add at least two weeks of buffer beyond the quoted transit time.
