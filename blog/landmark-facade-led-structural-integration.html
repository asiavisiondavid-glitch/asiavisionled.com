Landmark Facade LED Integration: Structure, Weight and Wind

2026-10-13United StatesGuide

By David

Once a display is attached to a building it stops being equipment and becomes part of the structure. That single change brings the display into the world of loads, movement and service access — and it means the screen cannot be engineered by the screen supplier alone.

This guide covers what a facade integration asks of the building, and where the display manufacturer's responsibility ends.

Wind Is Usually the Governing Load

On an exterior face, wind acts on the whole screen area rather than only on the frame, and its effect grows with height and exposure. For a large facade display it is commonly the load that sizes the attachment.

The magnitude is not a manufacturer's figure — it is calculated by the project's structural engineer against the code that applies to the site, using the local wind speed, the building height and the terrain. The manufacturer's contribution is the screen's geometry, its drag characteristics as a surface, and the position of its mounting points, which the engineer needs to run the calculation.

Where the split falls: the manufacturer supplies weight, dimensions and mounting-point data; the structural engineer designs the attachment and signs it off. A supplier that tries to specify the steel is outside its remit, and a project that expects it to is exposed.

Attachment Through a Secondary Frame

A facade display is normally carried by a secondary steel frame rather than fixed directly to the cladding.

The frame spans between the screen's mounting points and the building's structure, so the load path runs into the primary frame instead of into the facade panels. On a designed curtain wall this interface has to be coordinated with the facade consultant, because the display changes both the loads and the appearance at that plane.

The result is a three-way conversation between the display manufacturer, the facade engineer and the structural engineer, and it works best when the display's interface data is available before the frame is designed rather than after.

Thermal Behaviour and Movement

An exterior display sees daily and seasonal temperature swings, and the materials in the screen and the frame move at different rates.

The attachment therefore has to accommodate movement rather than lock the screen rigidly, so that thermal expansion does not load the modules or the frame. On a dark facade the screen also runs warmer than the cladding around it, which affects the cooling path and the temperature of the adjacent materials.

Water, Drainage and Ventilation

An exterior screen is a surface that rain will run over and wind will drive against, and the detailing has to direct water away from the sealed areas rather than into them.

The screen also has to breathe. Cabinets that are sealed against water still reject heat, and the ventilation path — whether passive or fan-assisted — needs to be designed so that the rejection does not pull water in with the air. This is a detail to settle at design stage, not to adjust on site.

Service Access at Height

On a facade, the maintenance method is a structural and architectural decision as much as an operational one.

Front-accessible modules allow a panel to be changed from the face of the screen, which avoids bringing a machine to the wall. Catwalks and internal walkways give permanent access at the cost of structure and space. Building maintenance units and rope access serve the screen without internal provisions, at the cost of cost and weather dependence.

Whichever route is taken, it should be settled with the design, because it affects the frame, the building section and the future operating cost of the installation.

FAQ

Q: Does a facade LED screen have to be engineered?
A: Yes. Once a display is attached to a building it is a structure, and its attachment has to be designed for the loads it will see and reviewed by the project's structural engineer. The manufacturer supplies the data; the engineer performs the design and signs it off.
Q: What loads matter most on a facade screen?
A: Wind is usually the governing load on an exterior face, especially at height, and it acts on the whole screen area rather than only on the frame. Self-weight, seismic demand and thermal movement also have to be accommodated. The magnitude is calculated by the structural engineer against the code that applies to the site.
Q: How is a facade screen attached to a building?
A: Commonly through a secondary steel frame that spans between the screen's mounting points and the building's structure, so the display is not carried directly by the facade cladding. On a curtain wall the interface is coordinated with the facade consultant.
Q: How is a high facade screen serviced?
A: The service method is a design decision, not an afterthought. Options range from front-accessible modules and catwalks to building maintenance units and rope access, and each has a different cost and a different effect on the structure. It has to be settled with the design.
