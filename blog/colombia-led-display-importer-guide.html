What does a Colombia LED display importer need to know? Importing LED displays into Colombia means registering with DIAN, clearing goods through Buenaventura or Cartagena, confirming whether the RETIE technical regulation applies, and specifying equipment for a tropical climate and a 110 volt grid. This 2026 guide explains the process.

Colombia is one of the larger LED display markets in South America, with demand from retail, banking, transport, stadiums, and events. Almost all professional screens are imported from China, and the process rewards buyers who prepare the paperwork before dispatch.

A Colombia LED display importer who confirms the registration and the conformity position in advance clears cargo without difficulty. The two factors that most often delay a project are the technical regulation question and the 110 volt supply.

Customs Entry for a Colombia LED Display Importer

Importers in Colombia register with the national tax and customs authority, DIAN, and operate under a tax identification number that identifies the entity filing the declaration. Foreign trade operations are declared through the DIAN customs system, and the importer carries legal responsibility for the accuracy of the entry.

A licensed customs agency files the declaration on behalf of the importer. The entry states the classification, the customs value, and the origin of each line, and duty and value added tax are assessed on that basis.

Work with a customs agency that handles electronic goods. The classification of a display, its power supplies, its control cards, and its mounting steel differs, and each line carries its own tariff treatment and its own conformity position.

RETIE, ONAC, and Colombian Product Compliance

Colombia regulates electrical products through technical regulations, of which the electrical installations regulation known as RETIE is the most relevant for LED displays and their power supplies. Standards are developed by Icontec, the national standards body, and certification bodies are accredited by ONAC, the national accreditation body.

Market surveillance is carried out by the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio, which can require evidence of conformity for products sold in Colombia. Confirm the position for the specific model rather than assuming a general rule applies, because the scope depends on the product and its use.

Keep the technical file after the goods are sold, because the authorities may request it during the life of the product and the same file supports a warranty claim. A file assembled at the order stage costs far less than one reconstructed later.

Duty, IVA, and the Landed Cost

Colombia applies a tariff that is low or zero for many capital and industrial goods, with value added tax on top. Duty is calculated on the customs value, normally the transaction value adjusted to the agreed Incoterm, and a customs services charge also applies.

Cost ElementBasisNotes
Customs dutyCommodity code and valueLow or zero for many goods, confirmed per code

Value added tax is recoverable for a registered business, so the effect is on cash flow rather than on final cost. Duty and the services charge are not recoverable and belong in the cost calculation from the beginning.

Agree the Incoterm deliberately. Under FOB the buyer arranges the freight and controls the entry, while under CIF or DDP the supplier arranges more. The choice changes the duty base and determines who holds the customs records.

Working With a Colombian Customs Agency

Importers work through a licensed customs agency rather than filing directly. Choose an agency that clears electronic goods rather than general cargo, and confirm that it holds the licences required for the ports and the type of operation you use.

Give the agency the complete document pack before dispatch. A shipment that reaches Buenaventura or Cartagena with incomplete paperwork is held while the gap is filled, and storage charges accumulate for every day the container remains in the terminal.

Confirm the consignee details on the bill of lading against the importer of record. A mismatch between the party named on the transport document and the party filing the entry is a common cause of delay, and correcting it takes longer than checking it in advance.

Specifying for Colombian Conditions

Colombia has a tropical climate that varies sharply with altitude. The Caribbean and Pacific coasts are hot and humid, the interior highlands around Bogota are cool, and the grid operates at 110 volts. The same specification will not suit every site.

For Colombian sites, low winter temperatures make cabinet construction and sealing the deciding factors — the X Series uses standard 500×500mm cabinets with front/rear service, and the COB EF outdoor series is sealed against snow-melt and moisture ingress.

Confirm the voltage configuration in writing at the order stage. A screen built for a 220 volt supply and installed on a 110 volt grid will not run, and the correction costs far more after the cabinets have shipped.

Logistics and Lead Times to Colombia

Most LED display cargo reaches Colombia by sea, entering through Buenaventura on the Pacific or Cartagena and Barranquilla on the Caribbean. The route is long, and the total lead time includes production, export formalities, the sea leg, import clearance, and inland delivery.

Common Mistakes Colombian Importers Make

  • Ordering before the technical regulation position is confirmed
  • Specifying a 220 volt screen for a 110 volt site
  • Assuming one specification suits coast and highland alike
  • Declaring a mixed shipment under one classification
  • Leaving the customs services charge out of the landed cost
  • Failing to hold the technical file after delivery
  • Using a customs agency that does not clear electronic goods

Each mistake is inexpensive to avoid during planning and costly after the goods land. A Colombia LED display importer who confirms the compliance position, configures the voltage, and specifies for the site avoids most of them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What does a Colombia LED display importer need before the first shipment?
A: Register with DIAN and obtain a tax identification number, appoint a licensed customs agency, confirm whether a technical regulation applies to the specific model, and prepare Spanish labelling documentation.
Q: Does Colombia require certification for LED displays?
A: Colombia regulates electrical products through technical regulations, of which RETIE applies to electrical equipment and installations. Certification is issued through bodies accredited by ONAC, and the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio carries out market surveillance.
Q: What is the mains voltage in Colombia?
A: Colombia operates at 110 volts, so power supplies must be built for that voltage rather than the 220 volt configuration used in many other markets. Confirm the configuration in writing at the order stage.
Q: How is duty calculated on imported LED displays?
A: Duty is calculated on the customs value using the commodity code, and rates are low or zero for many goods but must be confirmed per code. Value added tax and a customs services charge apply on top.
Q: What specification suits Colombian conditions?
A: The coast is hot and humid while the highlands are cool, so confirm the site. Coastal sites need sealed cabinets and a high ingress rating, and all installations need power supplies matched to 110 volts with surge protection.
