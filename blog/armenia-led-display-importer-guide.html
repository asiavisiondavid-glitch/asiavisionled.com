What does an Armenia LED display importer need to know? Importing LED displays into Armenia means importing into the Eurasian Economic Union, so the product must carry EAC conformity, and the cargo must transit through Georgia or Iran because Armenia has no seaport. This 2026 guide explains the certification, the transit routes, and the specification points for a continental climate.

Armenia is a small but active market for professional LED displays, with demand from retail, banking, hospitality, events, and public sector projects in Yerevan and the regional centres. Because the country has no coastline, every shipment arrives overland or by air through a neighbouring state.

An Armenia LED display importer who arranges EAC conformity and plans the transit route before ordering clears cargo without difficulty. The two issues that catch buyers out are the technical regulation documents and the fact that transit time is governed by a land border rather than a port schedule.

Customs Entry for an Armenia LED Display Importer

Customs clearance is administered by the State Revenue Committee. Armenia applies the Eurasian Economic Union customs code, so the declaration, the tariff, and the rules of origin follow the union framework rather than a separate national regime.

The importer needs a taxpayer identification number and a registered business address, and clearance is normally handled by a licensed customs representative. Entries are lodged electronically through the national customs system.

Confirm the transit route and the border crossing before the goods are dispatched. The Georgian route and the Iranian route have different transit times, different documentation, and different risk profiles in winter, and the choice affects the whole delivery schedule.

EAC Standards and Compliance

Armenia has been a member of the Eurasian Economic Union since 2015, and product conformity is demonstrated through the EAC system. LED displays and their power supplies fall under the union technical regulations for low voltage equipment and for electromagnetic compatibility.

Conformity is evidenced either by an EAC certificate or by an EAC declaration of conformity, depending on the product and the regulation. The documentation is registered in the union system, and the goods carry the EAC mark. The national standards body, the Armenian National Body for Standards and Metrology, represents the country in the union framework.

Confirm that the certificate or declaration is registered in the union system and names the exact model being supplied. A document that covers a different model in the same range does not satisfy the requirement, and the mismatch is usually found during clearance rather than by the supplier.

Duty, VAT, and the Landed Cost

Armenia applies the common Eurasian Economic Union customs tariff, and value added tax at the national standard rate on the duty-paid value. Because the country is landlocked, freight is a larger share of the landed cost than it would be for a coastal destination.

Build the landed cost from the delivered price rather than the factory price, because the transit leg through Georgia or Iran is a substantial fixed addition. For a heavy cabinet shipment, freight can be a meaningful percentage of the total, so it belongs in the comparison from the start.

Working With an Armenia Customs Representative

Clearance is normally handled by a licensed customs representative, and for a landlocked import the freight forwarder is often as important as the broker. The forwarder arranges the ocean leg, the transit through the neighbouring country, and the border formalities.

Choose a forwarder who runs the route regularly and who can provide the transit documentation and a realistic transit time. A forwarder who occasionally handles the corridor will underestimate the border time and the cost of the waiting days.

Ask for the transit plan in writing, including the named border crossings and the fallback route. Mountain roads and winter weather can close the preferred corridor, and a forwarder with a contingency plan delivers while others wait.

Specifying for Armenian Conditions

Armenia has a temperate continental climate with cold winters, hot dry summers, and large temperature swings, and much of the country sits at high altitude. Outdoor screens face freezing temperatures in winter, intense sun in summer, and dust carried on dry winds.

For Armenian sites, low winter temperatures make cabinet construction and sealing the deciding factors — the X Series uses standard 500×500mm cabinets with front/rear service, and the COB EF outdoor series is sealed against snow-melt and moisture ingress.

Armenia lies in a seismically active region, so mounting design deserves an engineering review for any large outdoor installation. The structural requirement is a local decision, but the supplier should provide the cabinet weights, the fixing points, and the load data the engineer needs.

For indoor installations the requirements are less demanding, though voltage stability and the quality of the mains supply should still be checked. Specify a wide-range power supply where the site experiences fluctuation.

Common Mistakes Armenia Importers Make

  • Ordering before the EAC documentation is confirmed for the model
  • Assuming a certificate for one model covers the whole range
  • Leaving the transit route and border crossing undecided at dispatch
  • Underestimating freight for a landlocked destination
  • Specifying a mild-climate cabinet for a high-altitude site
  • Ignoring seismic and snow load in the mounting design
  • Forgetting the transit documentation in the cost and schedule

Each of these is cheaper to fix before dispatch than at a border post. An Armenia LED display importer who confirms the EAC documents, plans the transit route, and specifies for the winter avoids most of them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does Armenia require EAC certification for LED displays?
A: Yes. Armenia is a member of the Eurasian Economic Union, so LED displays and their power supplies must demonstrate conformity under the union technical regulations through an EAC certificate or declaration, and the goods carry the EAC mark.
Q: How do goods reach landlocked Armenia?
A: Cargo normally arrives by sea at a Georgian port such as Poti or Batumi and crosses overland, or arrives through Iran and crosses at the southern border. The route and the border crossing should be fixed before dispatch.
Q: Which body represents Armenia in the standards system?
A: The Armenian National Body for Standards and Metrology represents the country in the regional standards framework, while EAC conformity documents are registered in the Eurasian Economic Union system.
Q: What is the customs authority in Armenia?
A: Customs clearance is administered by the State Revenue Committee under the Eurasian Economic Union customs code, with entries lodged electronically by the importer or a licensed representative.
Q: What specification suits Armenian sites?
A: Specify cold-rated components and cables for winter, a stated minimum operating temperature, thermal design for high-altitude sun, filtration for dust, and mounting data suitable for seismic and snow load review.
