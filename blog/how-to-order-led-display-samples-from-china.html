What is an LED display sample order? A sample order is a small purchase of one or two LED cabinets from a Chinese factory so the buyer can test build quality, brightness, color, and compatibility before committing to a full production order. Ordering LED display samples correctly turns a factory quote into real evidence. This 2026 guide covers which models to order, how to test them, and how sample costs translate into order credit.

Sample orders protect buyers from the two biggest import risks: the product does not match the quote, or the factory cannot produce consistently. A single cabinet is enough to evaluate pixel pitch, brightness, refresh rate, and cabinet construction. For complex systems, order the cabinet plus the receiver card and power supply that the full order will use.

What to Ask Before Ordering Samples

Before paying for a sample, ask the factory for the exact production specification: model number, LED brand, pixel pitch, module size, cabinet size, power supply, receiver card, and brightness bin. The sample must match the spec sheet attached to the quote. If the factory sends a sample with different components, the full order will follow the same pattern.

  • Request the production specification, not a brochure
  • Confirm the sample model matches the future order model
  • Ask whether sample value is credited against the first order
  • Set the sample delivery terms and address
  • Ask for test patterns and a calibration file with the sample

Sample Evaluation Checklist

Test the sample with your own content:
✅ White screen for uniformity and color temperature
✅ Gray ramp for banding and low-brightness steps
✅ Fast-moving video for refresh and ghosting
✅ Close-up photos for pixel defects
✅ Power supply temperature after 2 hours
✅ Cabinet alignment and seam quality
✅ Receiver card and control software compatibility

Use your own media player and content format if possible. A sample that only works with the factory's test laptop tells you nothing about your control environment. Record every test result and share it with the factory before the production order so the baseline is documented.

Production vs Sample Consistency

The most common disappointment is a beautiful sample followed by an inconsistent production batch. Prevent this by asking the factory to keep the sample cabinet as the golden reference and to calibrate production modules against it. Agree that production modules must use the same LED bin, driver IC, and power supply as the sample.

A second safeguard is ordering the sample from the same production line that will build your order. Ask for the production line name and include it in the purchase order. Factories with separate export lines sometimes produce better samples than the standard line, which is a signal you should not ignore.

Sample Costs and Credit Terms

Most Chinese LED factories charge for samples because a custom-built cabinet has real material cost. Standard practice is to credit the sample value against the first full order, which keeps the arrangement fair. Ask for this credit in writing on the quotation before paying.

Freight is where sample budgets grow. Air freight for one or two cabinets is fast but expensive; sea freight is cheaper but adds weeks. For evaluation, many buyers choose air freight and accept the cost because the information arrives sooner. The quote comparison guide explains how freight terms change the comparison.

Sample Test Environment Setup

Test the sample in an environment similar to the final installation. Indoor corporate screens should be evaluated in a room with controlled lighting; outdoor screens need daylight testing or a bright area that approximates direct sun. Keep the sample running for several hours, because brightness drift and power supply heat appear over time, not in the first ten minutes.

Use your own media player, content files, and control software. Prepare a test set with a corporate logo, white field, gray ramp, fast-moving video, and small text. The goal is to answer one question: can this cabinet run the content you plan to show every day?

Evaluating Brightness and Color in Practice

Measure brightness with a luminance meter after warm-up and compare it with the factory specification. White balance should be checked in the ambient light of the final venue, not in a dark factory room. Take photos and videos with fixed camera settings so you can compare the sample against future production modules.

Gray scale evaluation is more important than most buyers realize. A screen that handles video well but shows banding on gray content will look poor for corporate backgrounds and presentations. Run the gray ramp slowly and record where banding appears.

  • Photograph white, gray, and colored test patterns
  • Record video with fast motion to check refresh
  • Measure cabinet temperature after two hours
  • Test front and rear service access
  • Check the calibration file and software compatibility

Turning Sample Feedback into Order Terms

After the sample passes, send the factory an evaluation report and ask it to confirm that production will use the same components, calibration baseline, and quality checks. Keep the sample cabinet as the golden reference so the factory can compare production modules against it during assembly.

Write the sample credit into the quotation before ordering production: the sample value, the cabinetry specification, and the credit terms. A clear paper trail protects both sides and makes the full order faster to approve.

Production order terms to confirm:
✅ Same LED brand and brightness bin
✅ Same driver IC and power supply
✅ Same module and cabinet dimensions
✅ Calibration against the golden sample
✅ Sample credit applied to the first order

Sample Timing and Seasonality

Sample evaluation takes time, and buyers usually underestimate it. Between ordering, production, freight, and testing, a sample cycle can take three to six weeks. If you are planning an event or a building opening, start the sample process at least two months before the production order so the results inform the final specification instead of forcing a rushed decision.

Peak season changes sample speed too. Before major trade shows and in the months before Christmas installation windows, factories give samples lower priority than production orders. Order samples in quiet months when you can, and ask the factory for the sample production slot in writing so the date does not slip.

What Good Sample Support Looks Like

A factory that supports samples well answers technical questions within one business day, sends the specification with the sample, includes calibration and software files, and accepts a clear return or evaluation process. Weak sample support predicts weak order support: if the sales team disappears after payment for one cabinet, imagine the follow-up needed for a full wall.

Good sample support indicators:
✅ Specification sheet attached to the sample
✅ Calibration file and software provided
✅ Technical questions answered within one day
✅ Sample credit terms written on the quote
✅ The sample model matches the production model

Sample feedback should also cover logistics. Note how the cabinet was packed, whether the foam protected the corners, and how long delivery took. The same packaging standard should appear in the production order, because freight damage is one of the most common reasons screens arrive with bent frames or cracked modules.

Sample evaluation should produce a written pass or fail decision with reasons. If the sample passes, the factory receives a clear order; if it fails, the buyer receives a clear explanation of what must change. This decision discipline keeps the sample step from becoming an open-ended relationship with no commercial outcome.

A written decision also protects the sample investment when the chosen factory changes production terms later, because the evaluation document becomes part of the order record.

FAQ

Q: How many LED display samples should I order?
A: One cabinet is usually enough to evaluate a standard series. Order two cabinets when you need to check seams, alignment, or rental stacking, and add the receiver card or power supply when the full system depends on them.
Q: Are LED display sample costs refunded?
A: Many factories credit the sample value against the first full order, but the policy must be in writing. Some factories charge non-refundable fees for heavily customized samples.
Q: Can I test an LED display sample remotely?
A: You can review test videos and a live video call, but final evaluation needs the physical cabinet with your content. Remote reviews are a screening step, not a replacement for the sample.
Q: How long does sample delivery take from China?
A: Production of a standard sample cabinet takes about 7-15 working days. Air freight adds 5-10 days, while sea freight can add 3-6 weeks depending on the destination.
Q: What if the sample does not match the quote?
A: Stop and document the difference in writing. A sample with different LEDs, power supplies, or cabinet parts is a red flag for the full order. Ask for a corrected sample or move to another factory.
