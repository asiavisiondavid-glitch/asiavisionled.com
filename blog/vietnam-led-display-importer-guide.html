By Cassie

What does a Vietnam LED display importer need to know? Importing LED displays into Vietnam means registering for import, declaring goods with the correct classification, handling VAT and duty, and specifying equipment for tropical heat and a long rainy season. This 2026 guide covers each step.

Vietnam has become a significant LED display market, driven by retail expansion, transport projects, and a growing events sector. The import process is documented and increasingly digital, and a buyer who prepares the paperwork before dispatch clears cargo without difficulty.

The climate is the second consideration. Vietnam combines high temperatures with high humidity and a pronounced rainy season, and electronics designed for a temperate market suffer early failures in that environment.

Import Registration for a Vietnam LED Display Importer

A Vietnam LED display importer needs a business registration that includes the import activity, a tax code, and registration with the customs authority. Certain goods require additional permits or a conformity declaration before clearance, and the requirement depends on the commodity code.

Confirm the requirements for the specific product before ordering. A screen and its components may fall into different categories, and one line may need a certificate that another does not.

StepWhat Is RequiredWho Handles It

Work with a customs broker who understands electronic goods, because the classification of displays, power supplies, control cards, and mounting steel differs and each line carries its own treatment.

Customs Valuation and Duty in Vietnam

Duty is calculated on the customs value, normally the transaction value adjusted to the agreed Incoterm. Vietnam applies a tariff schedule with rates that vary by commodity code, and value added tax applies on top at the standard rate.

Cost ElementBasisNotes
Import dutyCommodity code and valueRate varies by code
Value added taxValue plus dutyStandard rate applies
Broker feePer declarationVaries by broker
Port and storagePer day after the free periodMounts if documents are late
Inspection feeWhere quality inspection appliesBudget for the processing time
Inland transportBy distance and sizeConfirm delivery to site

Because several charges apply on top of duty, the landed cost can be meaningfully higher than the ex-works price. Model the full cost before comparing suppliers, and treat the customs value as the figure that drives most of the add-ons.

Confirm the valuation basis with the broker before the goods arrive. The declared value drives duty and tax, and a valuation that does not match the invoice invites a query that delays release.

Conformity and Product Requirements

Some electrical products entering Vietnam must be declared as conforming to the applicable standard, and the requirement depends on the product category and the current rules. Confirm whether an LED display falls under a mandatory declaration before ordering.

Ask the supplier for test reports and certificates before the order. A supplier who can provide evidence makes the conformity step straightforward, while one who cannot leaves the importer to obtain it after manufacturing.

RequirementWhat It CoversEvidence to Hold

Keep the conformity file after the goods are sold. Vietnamese authorities can request it during the life of the product, and the same file supports a warranty claim if a component fails.

Specifying for Vietnamese Conditions

Vietnam's climate combines high temperature with high humidity and a long rainy season, and the combination is harder on electronics than either factor alone. Corrosion affects connectors and exposed metal, while heat stresses power supplies and driver boards.

For Vietnamese sites, humidity and salt-laden coastal air make sealed cabinets the deciding factor: the COB EF outdoor series (IP65, 3000 nits) and the COB ZH touring series (sealed, 3400–4000 nits) are built for exactly this duty.

Choose cabinets with effective sealing and corrosion protection, and confirm the power supply's temperature and humidity rating. Coastal sites are more demanding than inland ones, because salt air accelerates corrosion.

ConditionRiskSpecification Response
Year-round humidityConnector corrosionSealed cabinets and contact treatment
Monsoon rainWater ingress on outdoor screensHigh ingress rating and drainage
High ambient heatThermal stress on power suppliesHigher temperature rating
Coastal salt airAccelerated corrosionCorrosion-protected hardware
Dust at inland sitesCooling path blockageFiltration and cleaning schedule
Unstable supply voltageDriver damagePower conditioning on site

Where the site supply is unstable, plan power conditioning as part of the installation rather than as an afterthought. Driver boards are the component most often damaged by voltage events, and the repair cost exceeds the cost of a conditioner.

Shipping Routes and Lead Times

Most LED display cargo reaches Vietnam by sea, with air freight reserved for urgent spare parts. Transit times vary by route and port, and congestion at major gateways can add days during peak periods.

StageTypical DurationWhat Can Delay It

Inland to port and export3 to 5 daysBooking availability
Sea freightVaries by routeRouting and congestion
Import clearance2 to 5 daysIncomplete documents

Build the schedule from the total figure rather than the sailing time. Production, export formalities, the sea leg, import clearance, and inland delivery all consume time, and each can delay the project if it is left out of the plan.

Common Mistakes Vietnamese Importers Make

  • Ordering before the conformity route is confirmed
  • Undervaluing the goods to reduce duty
  • Choosing a screen rated for a temperate climate
  • Declaring a mixed shipment under one commodity code
  • Underestimating the add-on charges on landed cost
  • Omitting the buffer for clearance from the project programme
  • Failing to keep the conformity file after delivery

Each mistake is inexpensive to avoid during planning and costly after the goods land. A Vietnam LED display importer who confirms the licence route, models the landed cost, and specifies for the climate avoids most of them.
