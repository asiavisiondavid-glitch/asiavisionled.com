What does a Greece LED display importer need to know? Importing LED displays into Greece means clearing goods through Piraeus or Thessaloniki as an EU entry, holding CE marking and a RoHS declaration, registering with the national producer register for waste electrical equipment, and specifying cabinets for a hot, salt-laden Mediterranean climate. This 2026 guide explains the process.

Greece is an EU member state and the southeastern gateway of the single market, with demand for LED displays from tourism, retail, shipping, transport, broadcasting, and public procurement. Almost all professional screens are imported from China, and the process rewards buyers who prepare the documentation before dispatch.

A Greece LED display importer who treats the shipment as an EU customs entry, prepares CE and RoHS evidence, and specifies for the coastal climate clears cargo without difficulty. The obligations that catch buyers out are the producer registration and the summer heat and salt specification.

Customs Entry for a Greece LED Display Importer

Greece clears containerised cargo through the port of Piraeus, one of the largest container ports in the Mediterranean, with Thessaloniki serving the northern region and Patras serving the west. Because Greece applies the Union Customs Code directly, an importer registered for EU VAT can enter goods in Greece and release them into free circulation anywhere in the union.

Customs formalities are administered by the Independent Authority for Public Revenue, and declarations are lodged electronically through the ICISnet customs system. The importer needs an EU VAT number and an EORI number before a declaration can be lodged.

Piraeus is also a major transshipment hub, so cargo space to Greece is generally easy to secure. The complication for Greek projects is onward distribution, because many installations are on islands and the final leg depends on ferry schedules and weather.

CE Marking, WEEE Registration, and Greek Market Rules

Greece applies EU product law without national deviation for most electrical goods. LED displays and their power supplies fall under the Low Voltage Directive, the Electromagnetic Compatibility Directive, the RoHS Directive, and the Ecodesign rules for external power supplies, and the CE mark is the visible result of that conformity.

Separately, Greece implements the WEEE Directive through national producer responsibility. A producer, which includes an importer that places equipment on the Greek market for the first time, must register in the National Producers Register maintained by the Hellenic Recycling Agency, known as EOAN, and join a collective compliance scheme. EOAN also maintains the register of authorised representatives for producers established outside Greece.

A declaration of conformity that names the exact model is the core document, supported by test reports for that model. Keep the technical file after the goods are sold, because the authorities may request it during the life of the product and the same file supports a warranty claim.

Duty, VAT, and the Landed Cost

Greece applies the EU common external tariff. Under the tariff schedules that follow the Information Technology Agreement, many display and information-technology products enter at a zero rate, but the classification must be confirmed for the exact product, because a screen sold as a video display and a screen sold as a signage unit can fall under different commodity codes.

Value added tax is charged on the customs value plus duty plus freight and insurance to the place of destination, and Greece also applies reduced rates in designated island departments that can change the calculation.

Import VAT is recoverable for a VAT-registered business, so the effect is on cash flow rather than on final cost. Duty, the broker fee, and the producer registration cost are not recoverable and belong in the landed cost calculation from the beginning.

Agree the Incoterm deliberately. Under FOB the buyer arranges the freight and controls the entry, while under CIF or DDP the supplier arranges more. The choice changes the duty base and determines who holds the customs records and the technical file.

Working With a Greek Customs Broker

Most importers file through a customs agent that is connected to the ICISnet system. Choose an agent that clears electronic goods rather than general cargo, because the classification of a display, its power supplies, its control hardware, and its mounting steel differs and each line carries its own treatment.

Give the agent the complete document pack before dispatch. A shipment that reaches Piraeus with incomplete paperwork accrues terminal storage charges while the gap is filled, and storage at a high-throughput Mediterranean hub is expensive.

Confirm the consignee named on the bill of lading matches the importer of record and the EORI number held. A mismatch between the transport document and the customs declaration is a common cause of delay, and correcting it takes longer than checking it before dispatch.

Specifying for Greek Conditions

Greece has a Mediterranean climate with hot, dry summers, mild wet winters, and high solar exposure. Coastal and island sites add salt air, and the country is seismically active, which affects mounting design as well as cabinet specification.

For Greek sites, humidity and salt-laden coastal air make sealed cabinets the deciding factor: the COB EF outdoor series (IP65, 3000 nits) and the COB ZH touring series (sealed, 3400–4000 nits) are built for exactly this duty.

Logistics and Lead Times to Greece

Common Mistakes Greece Importers Make

  • Importing without registering in the EOAN producer register
  • Treating a model-specific declaration of conformity as covering the whole range
  • Specifying a temperate-climate screen for an exposed coastal site
  • Reusing a standard mounting design on a seismically active site
  • Leaving island ferry costs and delays out of the project plan
  • Using a broker that does not clear electronic goods
  • Failing to hold the technical file after delivery

Each mistake is inexpensive to avoid during planning and costly after the goods land. A Greece LED display importer who registers as a producer, assembles the technical file, and specifies for heat and salt avoids most of them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What does a Greece LED display importer need before the first shipment?
A: Obtain an EU EORI number and Greek VAT registration, confirm the commodity code and duty position, hold the CE technical file for the offered model, and register in the National Producers Register maintained by EOAN before placing equipment on the market.
Q: Who maintains the WEEE producer register in Greece?
A: The National Producers Register for electrical and electronic equipment is maintained by the Hellenic Recycling Agency, known as EOAN. Producers established outside Greece appoint an authorised representative and join a collective compliance scheme.
Q: Which ports handle LED display imports into Greece?
A: Piraeus is the main gateway and one of the largest container ports in the Mediterranean. Thessaloniki serves the northern region and Patras serves the west, and island installations add a ferry leg to the final delivery.
Q: What specification suits the Greek climate?
A: Greece has hot dry summers and mild wet winters with strong solar exposure, so specify a high ambient temperature rating, UV-stable coatings, corrosion-protected hardware for coastal and island sites, and a high ingress rating for winter storms.
Q: Is import VAT recoverable in Greece?
A: Import VAT is recoverable for a VAT-registered business, so the main effect is on cash flow rather than on final cost. Reduced rates apply in designated island departments, and duty, broker fees, and the producer registration cost are not recoverable.
